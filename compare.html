Which model for which question?

Every model has a shape. Some reason hard, some write well, some know what happened this morning. Below is what each one is genuinely good at — and why Council's answer is to stop choosing.

Beginners pick one. Professionals pick the right one.

The honest version of this: for most everyday questions any frontier model is fine, and picking between them is wasted effort. The difference only starts to matter when the question is hard, expensive to get wrong, or sits squarely in one model's strength. That is the narrow band where choosing well pays off — and where a panel pays off more.

The three roles

Council does not just run three models. It gives each one a job, so the answers do not collapse into three versions of the same opinion.

The Advocate

The case FOR it

Argues the strongest honest version of yes, so the best case gets made properly rather than assumed.

The Critic

The case AGAINST it

Finds what breaks before you do. This is the seat most single-model answers are missing.

The Builder

How to make it BETTER

Turns the argument into a next step instead of leaving you with two opposed opinions.
