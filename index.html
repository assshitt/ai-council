// How it works

From question to verdict.

Four steps, in the same order, every time. The panel is picked for the kind of question you ask.

01

The gate

Council decides whether your question has one correct answer. Factual ones get a single line and an honest nudge, not a staged debate.

02

Three roles

Judgment questions convene a panel picked for the type of question: one argues for, one against, one works out how to make it better. They answer in parallel.

03

The chairman

A fourth model reads all three, picks the strongest reasoning and issues one verdict, with the disagreement left in view.

04

Verification

Planned for Plus: factual claims in the verdict get checked against an outside source. Not switched on in the free preview yet.

It argues with itself.

ClaudeThe case against

A drop year is a full year of pressure with no guarantee. Many repeaters plateau, and the cost is real: coaching fees, a year behind your batch, and your health. If the problem was consistency rather than knowledge, another year may not fix it.

Join the college you got into and prepare alongside, or set a hard checkpoint: if your mock rank is not clearly above your target by month five, switch to plan B instead of drifting.

Drop only if your mocks were rising and you can name exactly what you will do differently. Otherwise take the seat and keep your options open.

// What we hold to

We debate before we believe.

Council principle

“It knows when to shut up.”

Ask something with one correct answer and you get one line, not a staged debate. You are not billed for theatre.

Council principle

“It refuses to fake certainty.”

The same confident tone for a settled fact and a coin flip is the problem Council exists to fix.

Council principle

“Every question has three sides.”

For it, against it, and how to make it better. The verdict comes with the split left in view.
