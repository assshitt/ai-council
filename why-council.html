Why Council

You already double-check.
The problem is you double-check with the same model.

Ask one AI a hard question and you get one confident answer. Ask again and you get the same answer in different words. Confidence is not the same thing as being right, and a single model cannot tell you which one you are looking at.

What is missing right now

Problem 01

One voice, no dissent

A single model hands you its best guess in a settled tone. You never see the reasonable case against it, because nothing in the system is trying to make that case.

Problem 02

It agrees with itself

Asking a model to check its own work mostly produces agreement. It shares its own blind spots, so it cannot audit them.

Problem 03

Confidence is flat

The same certain tone covers a settled fact and a coin flip. You cannot tell them apart from the writing.

Problem 04

Facts and judgments get one treatment

"What year did that happen" and "should I leave my job" are different kinds of question. Most tools answer both in the same voice.

Why disagreement is the useful part

Most multi-model tools combine AIs to give you more answers. Council combines them to show you where they clash. When three models trained differently land on the same conclusion, that agreement means something. When they split, the split is the most valuable thing on the page: it tells you the question is genuinely hard and shows you exactly where the difficulty sits.

We do not smooth that into a tidy consensus. The verdict arrives with the disagreement visible underneath it.

How a question moves through Council

Step 1

The gate

Council decides whether your question has one correct answer. Factual ones get a single line and an honest nudge, not a staged debate.

Step 2

Three roles

Judgment questions convene a panel: one argues for, one against, one works out how to make it better. They answer in parallel, without seeing each other.

Step 3

The chairman

A fourth model reads all three, picks the strongest reasoning and issues one verdict with the reasoning attached.

Verification, and its limits

Factual claims inside the verdict are checked against an outside source. This matters more than it sounds. Three AIs agreeing is still three guesses that happen to match. Only an external check makes "is this actually right" an answerable question.

We are being precise on purpose. One model is a guess. Three models and a chairman is a better reasoned guess. Adding outside verification is the first point where correctness becomes checkable at all, and even then only for the parts that can be checked.

The research behind it

Comparing several answers to catch a wrong one is an established technique, not something we invented. It was tested properly at the CONVERSATIONS 2024 workshop on human-centred chatbots.

What that work found, and we would rather say it ourselves: it helps, but it is not magic. Accuracy swung wildly depending on the subject, and it worked far better on a narrow domain than as a general truth detector. That is why Council sticks to decisions rather than claiming to be right about everything.

We do not build models. We rent them, and put them into a structured argument. What we built is the argument.
